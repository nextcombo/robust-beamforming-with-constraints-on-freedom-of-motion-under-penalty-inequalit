# Robust Beamforming with Constraints on Freedom of Motion under Penalty Inequality

Introduction Beamforming is a fundamental technology in array signal processing, which utilizes spatial diversity to enhance the desired signal, suppress undesired interference and noise. Beamforming techniques have been widely applied in multi-channel signal processing areas such as wireless communication [4], microphone array speech processing [5], radar [6], sonar [7], medical imaging [8]. The key step in beamforming is to specify what is called the beamformer, which acts as a vector of complex coefficients to linearly combine the signals received by an array of elements. Over the past decades, various design criteria for beamformers have been extensively studied, which can be categorized into two types: data-independent and data-dependent. Data-independent beamformers [9-11] have limited performance because they do not exploit useful information from the signal environment. In contrast, data-dependent beamformers can provide optimal performance due to their adaptability to the signal environment, thus also known as adaptive beamformers. A representative example of an adaptive beamformer is the Minimum Variance Distortionless Response (MVDR) beamformer [12], which has been widely used in many applications due to its practicality.
However, the performance of the MVDR beamformer suffers degradation due to imperfect knowledge of the environment, such as inaccurate steering vectors (SV), limited number of snapshots, DoA errors, etc. Generally speaking, these deficiencies lead to two types of model uncertainties in the MVDR beamformer. One uncertainty is the mismatch between the target signal SV and the estimated covariance matrix of the interference plus noise. This will inevitably cause distorted target signal. Another uncertainty is the estimation error of the covariance matrix of the interference plus noise. It is caused by the finite number of signal samples, the presence of the target signal in the training samples, and the non-stationarity of the signal. This uncertainty not only leads to a decline in interference suppression performance but also causes distorted target signal if the target signal exists in the training signal. In response to these two uncertainties, many robust beamforming algorithms have been proposed and studied in recent years to mitigate the negative impact of one or both uncertainties.
Vorobyov [13] provides a comprehensive review of the principles of robust adaptive beamforming technology. This paper introduces several representative robust beamforming techniques.
To address the uncertainty in SV, the Linearly Constrained Minimization of Variance (LCMV) beamformer [14] imposes multiple undistorted constraints on the possible DOA of the target signal. Although the LCMV beamformer stabilizes the main lobe response of the target, it indeed exhausts DoF resources, which could otherwise be used to suppress interference and noise. When the enforced SV includes DOA errors, recent work [15] proposes a Bayesian approach to jointly estimate DOA and suppress interference and noise. Beamformers based on uncertainty sets [16-21] assume that mismatches in SV are located within bounded spherical or ellipsoidal regions, rather than forcing multiple equality constraints. The main lobe can be stabilized by enforcing constraints based on worst-case criteria [16-18] or by iteratively estimating SV from the uncertainty set [19]. Another way to mitigate the uncertainty in SV is based on intrinsic space beamforming [22-24], which modifies SV by projecting it onto the estimated signal-plus-interference subspace. Additionally, authors in Ruan and de Lamare [25] proposed methods based on orthogonal Krylov subspaces to estimate SV in the reduced-dimensional subspace. These subspace-based beamformers may suffer from noise corrosion when the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) is low.
To address the uncertainty in covariance matrices, several robustness techniques are proposed by reconstructing sample covariance matrices. A well-known technique is based on Diagonal Loading (DL) for beamformers [26-28], which modifies the sample covariance matrix by adding a diagonal matrix. Although DL beamformers only modify the covariance matrix, they can also mitigate the impact of SV mismatches [17]. However, when there are target signals in the training samples and under high Signal-to-Interference-plus-Noise Ratio (SINR), the performance of this algorithm may degrade. By leveraging prior knowledge of the array manifold, several beamformers based on covariance matrix reconstruction are proposed [29-32]. These beamformers reconstruct the interference plus noise covariance matrix and estimate the SVs of the target signal according to the assumed SV estimates. Note that the covariance matrix reconstruction process is based on integral operations over specified angle regions, which may require significant computational costs, especially for large arrays. Yang et al. [33] authors propose a model based on additive random matrices to reconstruct it. However, this work does not consider the SV mismatch issue.
This paper aims to study the design problem of robust beamformers and assumes that different directions of the assumed SVs are available. Moreover, we assume that a rough DoA estimation for each source can be obtained through appropriate DoA estimation algorithms. These basic assumptions can be satisfied in many beamforming applications such as radar [6] and microphone arrays [5]. By utilizing these prior knowledge, a penalty inequality constrained minimum variance (P-ICMV) beamformer formula is proposed, and an effective optimization algorithm is given to solve this formula. Our main contributions are:
(1) In the design of robust beamformers, the robustness against DoA errors, SV mismatch errors, and covariance matrix estimation errors commonly occurring in practical applications is considered simultaneously. The robustness against these errors is implemented through multiple inequality constraints, where the assumed SVs are used to control the spatial response in the direction of interest.
(2) Minimum-Maximum Penalty Criterion: Enforcing multiple inequality constraints under limited degrees of freedom poses a design feasibility issue, especially in multi-source scenarios. To achieve feasible beamforming designs under various robustness considerations, we propose a penalty criterion to intelligently distribute the limited array degrees of freedom. Specifically, spatial responses across all possible interference directions are penalized, enabling the P-ICMV beamformer to handle robustness against multiple sources.
(3) An Efficient Optimization Algorithm: The proposed P-ICMV formula is a convex second-order cone programming (SOCP), and we develop an iterative algorithm based on the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) with low complexity to solve it. Moreover, the development of optimization techniques for solving a class of complex SOCPs itself is interesting.

Conclusion: The design of robust beamformers always seeks to balance between robustness and beamforming performance, with the core issue being how to automatically obtain an appropriate balance under limited array degrees of freedom. This paper introduces a minimum-maximum penalty criterion to intelligently distribute the limited degrees of freedom. The proposed P-ICMV formula utilizes two types of inequality constraints to introduce robustness against various uncertainties and employs the minimum-maximum penalty criterion to handle degree of freedom restrictions. Several user-specified parameters are also used in the formula, providing a flexible mechanism to achieve different levels of robustness. Additionally, a low-complexity iterative algorithm is designed, which can efficiently compute the P-ICMV beamformer even in large-sized array cases. The P-ICMV beamformer can provide an effective robust solution for challenging applications where DoF is constrained and model parameter accuracy is uncertain. The ability to achieve different levels of robustness is demonstrated through simulations.
